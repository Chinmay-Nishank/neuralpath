## Intuition: Why Mean Latency Lies in Production

Suppose 99% of your requests finish in `80 ms`, while 1% stall for `12,000 ms` on cold retries. Your mean latency looks great at `199 ms`—yet **1 in every 100 users waits 12 seconds** (and multi-step agents hitting 10 sequential calls trigger a slow outlier nearly 10% of the time!).

Production observability logs structured traces across every span (`embed -> vector_search -> rerank -> llm_call -> tool_exec`), alerting on **P95/P99 latency**, **cost per solved task**, and **guardrail trigger rates**.

<Viz type="roc-pr" />

<Callout type="tip" title="Deterministic Fast Guardrails Before Slow LLM Judges">
  Run millisecond regex/keyword/schema checks (PII masks, max token caps, JSON schema validation) synchronously on the request path, while running deeper LLM-as-a-Judge audits asynchronously on sampled production traces.
</Callout>

<Callout type="math-deep-dive" title="Tail Latency Amplification in Fan-Out Pipelines">
  If an agent invokes $k$ parallel sub-calls each with independent P99 tail probability $0.01$, the chance that *at least one* call triggers a tail stall is:
  <Math tex="P(\text{at least 1 slow}) = 1 - (1 - 0.01)^k" display />
  At $k = 20$ parallel retrievals, nearly $18.2\%$ of user turns hit a tail outlier unless hedged timeouts or tight P99 SLAs are enforced!
</Callout>
