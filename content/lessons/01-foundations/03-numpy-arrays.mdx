## Intuition: Everything in Deep Learning Is a Shaped Array

Images are 3D arrays `(height, width, channels)`. Text batches are 3D tensors `(batch, seq_len, d_model)`. Mastering `shape` reasoning in NumPy gives you instant fluency in PyTorch and JAX.

<GlossaryTerm term="Broadcasting">Broadcasting</GlossaryTerm> lets NumPy combine arrays of different shapes by comparing dimensions right-to-left: two dimensions match if they are equal or one of them equals `1`.

<Callout type="warning" title="Silent Bug: Rank-1 Shapes (N,) vs. Column Vectors (N, 1)">
  Subtracting a `(N,)` vector from a `(N, 1)` matrix broadcasts into an `(N, N)` outer matrix instead of element-wise subtraction! Always check `.shape` or keep explicit dimensions with `keepdims=True`.
</Callout>

<Callout type="math-deep-dive" title="Broadcasting Alignment Rules">
  Given arrays `A` with shape `(32, 10, 64)` and `b` with shape `(64,)`, NumPy right-aligns shapes as:
  `(32, 10, 64)` and `(1, 1, 64)`. Dimensions of size `1` stretch virtually without allocating extra RAM.
</Callout>

# Batch of 3 samples, 4 class logits each
logits = np.array([
    [2.0, 1.0, 0.1, -0.5],
    [0.5, 2.5, 0.2,  0.1],
    [-1.0, 0.0, 3.0, 1.0],
])

# Subtract row-wise max for numerical stability: shape (3, 1) broadcasts over (3, 4)
shifted = logits - np.max(logits, axis=1, keepdims=True)
exp_vals = np.exp(shifted)
probs = exp_vals / np.sum(exp_vals, axis=1, keepdims=True)

print("Probabilities:\n", np.round(probs, 3))
print("Row sums:", probs.sum(axis=1))`}
