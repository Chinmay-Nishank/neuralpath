Machine learning models rarely guarantee absolute certainty. Classifiers output probabilities over labels; language models predict a probability distribution over the next token; diffusion models learn to reverse noisy probability distributions.

## From Bayes' Rule to Cross-Entropy

Bayes' theorem updates our prior belief $P(H)$ after observing evidence $E$:

<Math tex="P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H)\,P(H)}{P(E)}" display />

<Callout type="math-deep-dive" title="Entropy, KL Divergence, and Cross-Entropy Loss">
  For a true distribution $p$ and predicted distribution $q$:
  - **Entropy:** $H(p) = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i$ (minimum average bits needed under truth $p$).
  - **Cross-Entropy:** $H(p, q) = -\sum_i p_i \log q_i$.
  - **KL Divergence:** $D_{\text{KL}}(p \| q) = H(p, q) - H(p) \ge 0$.

  Because $H(p)$ is fixed for ground truth labels, minimizing cross-entropy loss is mathematically identical to minimizing KL divergence between predicted probabilities and true data.
</Callout>

def kl_divergence(p, q):
    p = np.asarray(p, dtype=float)
    q = np.asarray(q, dtype=float)
