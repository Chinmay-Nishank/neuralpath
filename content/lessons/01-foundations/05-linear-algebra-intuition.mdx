## Intuition: Matrices Are Space Transformers

Every neural layer computes $y = W x + b$. Rather than memorizing rows-times-columns mechanically, picture the columns of $W$ as the new landing spots for your unit coordinate axes $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$. Drag the matrix entries below to watch the unit square stretch, rotate, or collapse.

<Viz type="linear-algebra" />

<Callout type="note" title="Determinant = Area Scaling Factor">
  When $\det(A) = 0$, the transformation flattens 2D space onto a 1D line or point—meaning information is permanently lost and the matrix has no inverse.
</Callout>

## Dot Products & Cosine Similarity

The dot product measures how strongly two vectors align:

<Math tex="u \cdot v = \sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i = \|u\|\,\|v\|\cos\theta" display />

Normalizing by vector magnitudes yields **cosine similarity** $\cos\theta \in [-1, 1]$, which underpins semantic search, RAG retrieval, and attention scoring.

<Callout type="math-deep-dive" title="Eigenvectors & Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)">
  An eigenvector $v$ of matrix $A$ only stretches without rotating: $A v = \lambda v$. Real-world weight and data matrices are rectangular ($m \times n$), so **SVD** generalizes eigendecomposition as $M = U \Sigma V^T$, keeping the top-$r$ singular directions used in **PCA** and **LoRA** adapters.
</Callout>

def pairwise_cosine(A, B):
    A = np.asarray(A, dtype=float)
    B = np.asarray(B, dtype=float)
    A_norm = A / np.linalg.norm(A, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    B_norm = B / np.linalg.norm(B, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return A_norm @ B_norm.T`}
