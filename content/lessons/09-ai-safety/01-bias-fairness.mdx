## Intuition: Deleting "Zip Code" Doesn't Make a Model Blind

A naive fix for fairness is **"fairness through unawareness"**—dropping protected attributes like gender or race from the feature table. Yet correlated **proxy features** (ZIP codes, school names, purchasing habits, browsing timestamps) reconstruct protected attributes almost effortlessly!

Auditing requires measuring **outcomes** across groups:
- **Demographic Parity:** positive prediction rates $P(\hat{Y}=1 \mid G=a) \approx P(\hat{Y}=1 \mid G=b)$.
- **Equal Opportunity / Equalized Odds:** equal True Positive Rates (qualified individuals get approved equally) and equal False Positive Rates across groups.

<Viz type="roc-pr" />

<Callout type="math-deep-dive" title="Fairness Impossibility Theorem">
  Whenever two populations have genuinely different base rates $P(Y=1 \mid G=a) \neq P(Y=1 \mid G=b)$ and a predictor is imperfect, it is mathematically **impossible** to satisfy Calibration, Equal Positive Predictive Value, and Equalized Odds simultaneously. Domain ethics must explicitly choose which tradeoff fits the harm profile!
</Callout>

  exerciseSolution={`import numpy as np

def disparate_impact_ratio(y_pred, group_mask):
    yp = np.asarray(y_pred, dtype=float)
    gm = np.asarray(group_mask, dtype=bool)
    rate_unpriv = float(np.mean(yp[gm]))
    rate_priv = float(np.mean(yp[~gm]))
    return rate_unpriv / max(1e-12, rate_priv)`}
