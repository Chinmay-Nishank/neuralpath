## Intuition: The Forger vs. The Art Detective

A **Generator** $G(z)$ transforms random noise into synthetic samples trying to fool a **Discriminator** $D(x)$, while $D(x)$ learns to separate real training data from fakes. Unlike VAEs (which minimize blurry pixel averages), GANs learn perceptual realism automatically—at the cost of delicate saddle-point instability!

Common failure modes include **mode collapse** (the generator discovers one convincing sample—say, a single digit `"1"`—and ignores every other class) and saturating gradients when $D$ becomes too accurate too fast.

<Viz type="diffusion" />

<Callout type="math-deep-dive" title="Minimax & Non-Saturating Generator Objectives">
  Classic GAN optimization solves:
  <Math tex="\min_G \max_D \; \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))]" display />
  In practice, $G$ maximizes $\log D(G(z))$ (the *non-saturating heuristic*) so gradients remain strong early when fakes look obviously wrong.
</Callout>

def gan_losses(d_real_prob, d_fake_prob, eps=1e-9):
    d_loss = -np.mean(np.log(np.clip(d_real_prob, eps, 1)) + np.log(np.clip(1.0 - d_fake_prob, eps, 1)))
    # Non-saturating generator objective: maximize log D(G(z))
    g_loss = -np.mean(np.log(np.clip(d_fake_prob, eps, 1)))
    return float(d_loss), float(g_loss)

# At Nash equilibrium p_data == p_g, optimal discriminator outputs 0.5 everywhere!
print("Equilibrium (D=0.5):", [round(v, 4) for v in gan_losses(np.array([0.5, 0.5]), np.array([0.5, 0.5]))])
print("Overpowered D(fake)=0.01:", [round(v, 4) for v in gan_losses(np.array([0.99]), np.array([0.01]))])`}
